## 2. GT-6's pilot — the split proof

### 2.1 Why the proof is a GT-delta and not a prim hash

GT-6's row states its own acceptance test as a *"prim-hash / **GT-delta** diff"* that must come
back empty except the enumerated landing-top rows. It also states, in the same breath, why the
prim-hash half cannot be the instrument **across the mesh flip**: *"Converting Cube → Mesh
necessarily rewrites the inventory, so a default-ON conversion would make GT-6's own proof
impossible to run"* `[cite]`. Turning `mesh=` on is exactly that conversion. So the prim hash is
used where it is meaningful — **unscoped invariance**, §7.2 — and the walked surface is measured
directly for the flip itself.

### 2.2 The instrument

`gt_probe.py` (GPU 0) installs the same fake `pxr`/`omni` modules
`scripts/geom_invariance_check.py` uses, runs the scene's `main()` on the capture path, then
reconstructs **every** solid from the recorded prim table — every `Cube`, whatever code path made
it (`add_box`, `_oriented_box`, `build_arc_steps`'s *inline* box branch, scene-local helpers),
rebuilt from its own translate / rotZ / rotX / scale ops against the library's fixed `size = 2.0`
convention; `_annular_sector_mesh` is additionally wrapped so Mesh sectors carry exact
`(r_in, r_out, a0, a1, z_top)` instead of an AABB. It then computes the **highest solid top face**
analytically on a 9-radius × 1969-azimuth grid over the spiral annulus and the landing
(**17,721 samples**, azimuth −6°…486° at 0.25°, radii 1.55…3.25).

> The first version of this probe wrapped `_oriented_box` and missed the landing entirely,
> because `build_arc_steps`'s box branch calls `UsdGeom.Cube.Define` inline rather than going
> through `_oriented_box`. That is recorded because it is the kind of hole that makes a proof
> read green while measuring nothing.

Both arms are isolated `git archive` trees (`assets/` symlinked in — see §7.3), so the seven
other lanes sharing this worktree cannot leak into either side.

### 2.4 What the azimuth clip does and does not fix

The clip azimuth is exact: the deck edges `x = 2.0 / 5.0` meet the landing rim `r_out = 3.30` at
`acos(1.5/3.30)` = **62.964°**, so the landing becomes `[0, 62.964]` ∪ `[117.036, 180]`.

**No hole is opened.** Every point of the removed wedge satisfies `|x − 3.5| ≤ 1.5` and is
therefore deck, at the *same* 5.000 z — which is why the clip and the +2 mm had to land together:
the 2 mm offset existed only to buy z-fighting immunity where the two were coplanar in plan, and
the clip removes exactly that region. The probe confirms it: `new-void samples 0`.

**Honest limit.** The clip removes the central wedge (5.030 m²) but leaves the two slivers where
a lobe still passes under the slab at r < 3.30. Interpenetration **9.186 → 4.156 m², −54.8 %**
`[measured]`. Closing the rest needs either a deck south-end move to `y = −10.06` (the landing
arc's minimum y over the deck width) or a non-annular landing primitive. **The deck move was
designed and then rejected**: `_grid_shift()` derives the judge-grid origin from `deck["y0"]`, so
moving the deck moves every `preset_*` camera, and §7 ruling 7's principle is explicit —
*legibility comes from geometry, never from camera edits*. Decoupling `_grid_shift` from the deck
(the origin is a *spiral* property: it is numerically `sp["cy"]`) would make the move camera-safe
and is the recommended route, but it is a camera-adjacent refactor and this lane did not take it
unilaterally. Filed in §8.

## 3. Identity — the 06/11 split (R06-2)

### 3.1 Bronze horizontal tube, 4 rails

`props_kit.build_tube_railing`'s statutory note is carried into the scene rather than
paraphrased: a horizontal-rail guard does **not** satisfy the 안목 ≤ 100 mm rule the way a
baluster guard does, and the compliant form is **4 rails inside 1.10 m**, largest clear span
`(1.10 − 0.12)/3 − 2(0.024)` = **0.279 m** — a *deliberately* non-baluster product. The scene now
matches that arithmetic exactly, and does not blend the two vocabularies.

**The hazard is untouched.** Over the damaged arc (azimuth 180–270°) **every** rail is missing and
the posts remain, as the brief specifies. Going 2 rails → 4 makes the intact 210° *more*
convincing, which sharpens the misread rather than softening it: the cue is the **absence**, not
the count. The scene's own smoke re-derives it — open edge z +4.904…+3.406 over ground −0.005,
drop **4.909…3.411 m, mean 4.160 m** `[measured]`.

Colour moved from teal `(0.045, 0.105, 0.115)` — the generic municipal guardrail tone 06 and 11
*shared*, i.e. precisely the overlap the user named — to bronze `(0.255, 0.150, 0.082)`, kept one
notch warmer and lighter than `wood_color` so tube and timber do not read as one material.

### 3.2 V-form pillars

Each support is a footing pad plus two legs; each leg is 5 stacked cylinders on a linear radius
taper 0.30 → 0.17 (a `Cylinder` cannot taper), tilted about Y.

**The splay opens across the deck (±X), not along it**, for two measured reasons: (a) the V then
reads frontally, which is the axis `overview`, `ground_approach` and `ground_graze` actually look
down; (b) a ±0.95 m splay along Y would push the south leg head to `y = −9.95`, i.e. **0.25 m
inside the spiral's `r_out` 3.30 plan footprint** — there is only 0.70 m of clearance there
(centre distance 4.000 − 3.300). Across the deck the leg heads sit at x 2.55 / 4.45, inside the
3.0 m deck width and 4.11 m from the spiral centre. `_solid_at` carries one conservative envelope
over footing and both legs.

### 3.3 Glass on the approach span

G6 guards the deck with **laminated glass under a bronze tube rail**. The v6 build alternated
**opaque** sound-panel bays with open bar bays, for a stated reason: an unbroken panel made *"both
corridor walls pure black shadow faces"* once the grid moved onto the deck run. Glass satisfies
that reason **strictly better than an open bay does** — it is see-through over the *full* bay
rather than between bars — so every bay is now glazed rather than every other one: steel shoe kick
band, a 19 mm laminated panel with a real joint at each post, bronze cap rail above.

`props_kit.build_glass_balustrade` is the right template and its three-part form (shoe / jointed
panel / capping rail) is what is built, but it could not be **called**: its capping rail uses the
same `rotY=90` that restricts `build_tube_railing` to X-aligned runs, and the deck is Y-aligned
(finding S06-F1). The panel joint is the whole read — a single continuous sheet is the give-away
of a procedural balustrade — so the joints are modelled, not textured.

This is a **guard-height** change: it contributes one stratum to the probe (`deck → guard`,
+1010.000 mm, 44 samples) and touches no walked surface.

### 4.1 Timber road guardrail — and a kit finding

`props_kit.build_tube_railing` is called for real here, on an **X-aligned** run along the south
kerb, `rails=3`, 28 posts, `clear_max` **0.210 m**. `rails=3` is the template's own allowance for

reflective bands are modelled as **post wraps**, which is what a Korean 차선분리 guardrail
actually carries — they are not painted on the rails.

> **Finding S06-F1 (props_kit, MED).** `build_tube_railing` lays every rail with `rotY=90.0`,
> which puts the tube along **+X** regardless of the polyline's bearing; the bearing `ang` is
> computed and then `del ang`. Its docstring concedes the axis-aligned case "is the only case
> shipped", but the restriction is stronger than that reads: a **Y-aligned** polyline silently
> produces rails crossing the run. This is why the deck's bronze rail and the helix's rails are
> built in-scene against the template's numbers instead of calling it. Kits are frozen this
> window; filed for the K-lane, not patched.

## 9. Two pilot renders spent on the backdrop, and what they bought

Recorded because the outcome was a **revert**, and a revert that is not written down reads later
as an omission.

G6's backdrop is a forested hill, a low-rise village and open sky; BS-4's instruction is that the
far tier *must not close the horizon*. HEAD closed it completely — six blocks of 13.5–24 m whose
brick face filled the whole background of `overview` and most of `spiral_up`.

- **Attempt 1** raised the existing `treeband` in place (4.4/4.8 → 8.8/9.6 m at y ∓33…40). The
  render showed a row of green monoliths with dome caps: a wall of vegetation replacing a wall of
  brick, which is not an improvement.
- **Attempt 2** pushed it to y ∓46…54 and widened the segment 7 → 14 m. Still a wall — a 10–11 m
  band at 33–41 m subtends ~17°, and the `h1.8/d10` eye sits 5 m up on the deck looking into it.
- **Landed**: the band is returned to **exactly its v6 numbers**, where it sits below the deck eye
  and does only the job it was designed for (stopping the grass slab meeting the sky). The horizon
  is opened the **other** way and only that way: the village blocks drop **13.5–24 m → 6.0–9.0 m**
  (2–3 storeys) and retreat to y ∓66…82 / x ∓104…120.

**What is therefore NOT delivered: G6's forested hill.** It needs a real landform — a displaced
ground mesh or a ridge billboard — not a row of boxes. It is left undone and named, rather than
faked with a taller band.

## 10. Findings

| id | sev | finding |
|---|---|---|
| **S06-F1** | MED | `props_kit.build_tube_railing` lays every rail with `rotY=90.0`, i.e. along **+X**, regardless of the polyline's bearing (`ang` is computed and then `del ang`). The docstring concedes the axis-aligned case "is the only case shipped", but a **Y-aligned** polyline silently produces rails crossing the run rather than following it. Used correctly here on the X-aligned road guardrail; the deck's and helix's bronze rails are built in-scene against the template's numbers because they are Y-aligned and curved. `build_glass_balustrade` has the same restriction in its cap rail. Kits frozen — filed for the K-lane |
| **S06-F2** | MED | `SHRUB_SPECIES["riparian"]` names `Shrub/Switchgrass.usd`, which **exists on disk but is absent from `VEG_SHRUBS`** — the table `place_shrubs` filters every candidate through. The role therefore resolves empty and falls back **silently** to `SHRUB_ORNAMENT`, returning a healthy count for a bed that is not what was asked for. The first render of this scene put two Rhododendron beds side by side while the scene printed "grasses 14". `pool=` is filtered through the same table, so no caller-side workaround exists. `verge_turf` / `edge_weed` (`Grass_Short_A/B/C`) are in the same position. **Consequence for W3**: any scene that asked for grasses or turf through a role has silently got ornament shrubs |
| **S06-F3** | LOW | `_grid_shift()` derives the judge-grid origin from `deck["y0"]`, so the deck's south end cannot move without moving every `preset_*` camera. The origin is conceptually a **spiral** property (it is numerically `sp["cy"]`); pinning it there would make the deck end movable and unblock the rest of the landing/deck de-interpenetration (§2.4). Camera-adjacent, so not taken unilaterally |
| **S06-F4** | LOW | A `git archive` arm is **not asset-equal** to the worktree (`assets/vegetation/` is gitignored). Any lane comparing linter counts or vegetation across such an arm must symlink `assets/` first, or it will read a phantom regression — this lane did, once (§7.3) |
