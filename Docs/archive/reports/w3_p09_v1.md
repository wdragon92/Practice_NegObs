## 2. Row (1) — 판석 디딤돌

### 2.1 Where, and why there

One route, in the **−Y lawn band**, `x = −14.6`, `y −12.42 … −17.55`, pitch **0.57 m**, stagger
**±0.10 m** alternating, **10 slabs**.

**The route has a destination, and that is the whole argument.** Boardwalk leg L1 runs
`x −16.0…−9.2` at `y −20.4…−18.0` — its landward end. Before this commit **the boardwalk began in
the middle of a lawn with no land approach at all**: you could reach it only by walking across
turf. A 디딤돌 line exists precisely where a desire line crosses turf between two hard surfaces,
and this is that line. The last slab stops **0.147 m** clear of L1's north edge in plan `[measured,
stepstone_selfcheck]` — a normal final stride onto a 0.06 m deck.

`x = −14.6` is **not** a round number and not the centre of anything. The obvious line is `x = −13`,
straight from the promenade to the middle of L1 — and the park tree at **(−13.0, −17.8)** stands on
it. The gate asserts ≥ 1.00 m from every trunk station; the binding pair measures **1.34 m**. The
number that made the choice is in the gate, so the choice cannot rot.

**No +Y mirror**, deliberately. The +Y lawn has no boardwalk, therefore no destination, and a
decorative copy of a functional route is the exact thing the user's ban is about.

### 2.2 What a slab is

| parameter | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| plan outline | **irregular 6- or 7-gon**, per-vertex radius drawn from **0.235–0.315 m**, per-vertex angle jittered ±12° | §7.2's *"prefer irregular outlines"*, met **by construction**. Angle jitter is capped well under half the 51.4°/60° vertex spacing, so the ring stays monotonic and the polygon cannot self-intersect |
| thickness | **0.120 m** | the 화강석 판석 100–150 mm band. Real relief, bedded into the turf — **not** a decal |
| top face | `lawn_proud 0.030 + 0.012` = **0.042** | **12 mm proud of the mown turf** |
| collider | **False** | the lawn box beneath is already `collider=True` with its top 12 mm lower |
| material | `plaza_light` at `stepstone_tint` = **0.92 × the paving tint**, texture scale 0.75× | a lawn-set stone is a different stone lot; 0.75× because a 0.55 m slab would otherwise carry less than half of the promenade's 1.1 m granite cell |
| prim | **one `UsdGeom.Mesh` per slab** — side quads + two n-gon caps, `subdivisionScheme="none"` | `sc.add_box` is a rectangle; `sc.add_disc` is a **regular** n-gon, i.e. a machined disc, which is a 원형 디딤돌 and not a 판석. The `build_hip_roof` in-scene mesh precedent, with the ring made irregular |

**Why 12 mm and not the 30–60 mm a Korean 디딤돌 usually stands proud.** Both numbers are real and
they belong to different situations. A 디딤돌 in a **planting bed** stands 30–60 mm proud — it has
to shed water and out-top the ground cover. A 디딤돌 in a **mown lawn** is set flush-to-slightly-
proud, because anything taller is a mower strike. G9's slabs sit in mown turf with grass to their
edges. So 12 mm is the correct detail here, and it is *also* the one that keeps the row honest:
**12 mm < `ground_kit.GT_DELTA` 0.020**, so no walked-surface change is claimed and none is needed.

### 2.3 The permission, as a gate rather than a paragraph

the same `stepstone_outlines()` the assembler extrudes — one source, the `hip_roof_topology`
convention. It **fails the build** on any violation, and it reads `GT_DELTA` from `ground_kit`
rather than hard-coding it, so a later change to that constant re-decides this scene automatically.

### 3.1 The arithmetic, and what rides with it

| tint | before | after | ratio to `stone_tint`, before → after |

| `stain_tint` (waterline band **and** the kit's joint lines) | (0.299, 0.299, 0.263) | **(0.245, 0.245, 0.215)** | 0.4672 → **0.4676** |

**Why four tints move when the ruling names one.** The ruling authorises *the lane's measured
correction*, and the lane's measured correction is `w3_s09_v1.md` §7 **P1**, which specifies
`stain` and `moss` scaling *"by the same 0.818 so the waterline contrast ratio — this scene's only
drop anchor — is preserved, exactly as v7 did it"*. The two dry-stone tints are **the same class of
consequence and are stated here rather than smuggled**: the file authors `drystone_tint` as *"0.86×
the paving tint"*. Left at 0.55 while the paving fell to 0.524, the field-stone wall would have come
out **lighter than the machined slab beside it** (ratio 1.050) — the exact inverse of the physical
claim the parameter is built on. The two dry-stone faces are outside the ±30° cone of all three
h0.3 presets, so they cannot enter §5's wht% verification in either direction; the two halves of
this row are cleanly separable and are separated here.

**Not scaled, deliberately**: `gk_crack`, a flat 0.055 near-black. A crack is not a ratio of the
stone it is in, and darkening the paving only *widens* its contrast.

`albedo_selfcheck` after the move: paving albedo **0.300 → 0.246**, predicted horizontal render
**192.7 → 176.1** sRGB. 0.246 is the middle of the real grey-granite 0.20–0.35 band rather than its
top. **0 large-area violations**, all 16 rows including the new `stepstone_tint` (0.226 / 169.6).

### 5.3 Why it stops there — measured, not guessed

P1's arithmetic (*"p95 0.8745 → 0.80 sRGB"*) assumes the near-band pixel is **pure diffuse from
this albedo**, in which case a linear ×0.818 is an sRGB ×0.9197. The measurement disagrees:

```
mean 203 → 195 sRGB  ⇒ linear 0.5970 → 0.5459  ⇒ measured linear ratio 0.9144
solve  f·0.818 + (1 − f)·1.0 = 0.9144   ⇒   f = 0.470
```

**Only 47 % of the linear radiance in the judged near band responds to the paving albedo.** The
other 53 % does not, which is why a tint can move `wht%` by 26 pp and `p95` by only 5 sRGB: the
bright tail is not albedo-limited, so tint alone cannot reach the band and no amount of further
darkening would get there without destroying the granite's identity.

**Where the other 53 % comes from is not a mystery, and it has a precedent in this very scene.**
`scene_common.LOOK_CLASS` (`scene_common.py:380-395`) authors a `spec` key for **`asphalt`** —
`spec=0.20` (`:394`), with the comment *"diagnosis P1/P3 — the road surface is excessively bright
because of grazing gloss, so specular_level is stated explicitly"* (`:391-393`) — and authors
**no `spec` at all for `stone`** (`:382`), which is the class this scene's paving resolves to (`[룩v1] … 역할 … stone:4`). With
`spec` absent, `_make_ground_pbr` never writes `specular_level_a` and OmniPBR's default 0.5
(F0 0.04) survives. That is **exactly the v8 Y1 mechanism** that turned this scene's own pavilion
roof near-white and was fixed there with an explicit `specular_level=0.0`. The same diagnosis was
applied to the road and never to the flagstone. → **P09-F1**, §6; `scene_common.py` is a frozen kit
this window, so it is routed and not touched.
