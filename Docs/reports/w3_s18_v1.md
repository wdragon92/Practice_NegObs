## 1. Why the sea was invisible — the diagnosis, and why the old number lied

The evidence is the baseline round itself:
`look_check/scene18/260730_w2d_fix/pt_noon_preset_h0.3_d5.png` and `…_sea_beauty.png`.
In both, the water **is geometrically in frame** and reads as a pale grey wash that the eye
takes for haze.

| # | cause | measured / reasoned |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | **The check measured the wrong quantity.** `_sea_selfcheck` swept ±30° of azimuth asking *"does a town box rise above the water's far edge?"*. Nothing did — v6 had already pushed the town off-axis — so it returned 54-100 % and stopped. It never asked how many pixels of the frame are water, nor whether water is distinguishable from sky. | the openness number and the user's sentence were never about the same thing |
| **C2** | **PHYSICAL CAUSE — the water was a sky mirror.** `water_rough = 0.22` on a smooth dielectric seen at **0.4-5° grazing incidence** is, by Fresnel, an almost perfect mirror, and under a bright noon puresky it returned the sky's own radiance. Water and sky rendered at nearly the same value, so **the horizon line disappeared**. | v6 water: albedo mean **0.202**, roughness **0.22**; the rendered band is 6 % of frame height at sky-like value |
| **C3** | **No shoreline event** — no surf, no foam, no wet-sand band. In G18 the sea is legible mainly through three white breaker lines and a wet swash band; v6 had a hard straight land/water joint and nothing else. | — |
| **C4** | **Composition.** The water band was ≈4.8° tall inside a −10° frame, sitting behind **37 m of grass**, which reads as a lawn running into haze. | eye h0.3 at d5: ground top −3.01, water top −3.35, shoreline 36 m out |

Note that C1 is not a bug in the arithmetic — the openness figure was true. It was an
**occlusion** test being read as a **legibility** test.

### 1.1 The fixes, in the same order

* **C1 → the instrument is replaced.** `_sea_selfcheck` **v2** rasterises the real frustum
  of every judged cut (hFOV 60°, vFOV 36° — the Isaac default 18.1476 mm / 20.955 mm
  aperture at 16:9) against an analytic model of every macro surface, and reports
  **per-class frame coverage %, the horizon row, and the fraction of frame WIDTH that still
  carries water**. It gates on `water % ≥ floor`, `horizon inside the frame`, and
  `open columns ≥ 35 %`. The last number is the direct form of v6's failure mode: a town
  wall hiding the horizon everywhere scores ~100 % on "openness" and near 0 % here.
* **C2 → `water_rough 0.22 → 0.38`, `specular_level 0.42`**, body colour from haze grey
  `(0.175, 0.205, 0.225)` to sea blue `(0.044, 0.112, 0.166)`. A far haze plate beyond
  1.2 km (`(0.105, 0.140, 0.176)`, roughness 0.52, 2 mm lower so the seam cannot z-fight)
  reproduces G18's horizon lightening **without** closing the sea/sky value gap.
* **C3 → a surf system**: four broken breaker lines, segments **overlapping** (`step` <
  drawn length) and each carrying a ±4° z-rotation so the line is neither dashed nor
  axis-aligned, two foam albedos (near 0.520 / far 0.360 — a real breaker loses contrast
  with distance), plus a damp swash veneer at the waterline.
* **C4 → the travel axis +X is now seaward and perpendicular to the shore**, so the sea
  fills the **full frame width** in every judged cut and the near field is sand, not lawn.

## 3. The sun — a second cause-first fix, found by the pilot

The first pilot (`260731_w3_s18`, round a) came back with the entire promenade and 18 m of
beach in shadow. Cause: `SUN_AZ_OFFSET = 171.5` inherited from scene01 puts the sun at
**world az 33.5 + 171.5 = 205°** (the −X·−Y sky), so shadows propagate along az 25° (+X, +Y)
— straight over the promenade — and the 40-58 m coastal wall at x −26 throws a
`h / tan 49.79° = 49.1 m` shadow.

This is the **scene07 / scene10 precedent verbatim** (both moved their offset for exactly
this reason, and said so in the file). Two candidates were rendered, not assumed:

| offset | world az | shadow az | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 171.5 (inherited) | 205° | 25° | promenade + 18 m of sand in shadow — `pt_noon_sea_beauty.png` round a |
| 221.5 | 255° | 75° | promenade lit, but the revetment face and all 16 risers at `cos 255° · cos 49.79° = −0.167` → a **near-black flight** (`pt_noon_color_front.png` round b) |
| **251.5** | **285°** | **105°** | adopted |

At 251.5 the wall's shadow runs **landward** (Δx = 49.1·cos 105° = −12.7 m) and never
touches the promenade; the deck and sand are fully lit (normal +Z, cos 49.79° = 0.764); the
revetment face and the risers get a **grazing** `+0.167` — dim enough that the 2.56 m drop
keeps a dark band, bright enough that the flight is not a black hole; the two stringer
cheeks split lit/shaded (±0.62), which is what gives the flight its modelling. No sun glint:
the specular path off still water needs a 49.79° depression and no judged cut looks steeper
than ≈6.5°.

story in a comment so the next person who lengthens a plate does not repeat it.
