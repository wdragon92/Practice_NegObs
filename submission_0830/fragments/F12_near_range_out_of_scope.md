> The near field is outside this paper's claims, for two different reasons that should not be merged. First, at the regulation camera pose the 0–2 m band does not enter the frame at all: at 1.5–1.8 m height and a pitch of −15° to −3° in 16:9, only **15 of the 20 registered cells** are ever visible, so the real-world scoring denominator is 15 — a field-of-view constraint of the observation protocol, not a property of the models. Second, at those distances a rim-only hazard is geometrically impossible. Separately, and as a model property rather than a protocol one, the RGB polar head never fires a 0–2 m cell at the operating threshold, consistent with a training prior of 0.021 there against 0.24–0.36 in the far band.
>
> **The difficulty specific to drop-offs is the opposite one: the visible aperture vanishes as roughly 1/d².** The near-range answer is video propagation, which is future work.
